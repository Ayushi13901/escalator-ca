How the results are produced

What the model does, how PLOS is graded, and what the stand-only verdict means.

The cellular-automata model

The escalator is 2 lanes × N steps; one cell holds one person. Every 0.01 s the model (1) moves the belt one step every Sl/v seconds, (2) lets walkers climb one step at their own pace (inverse speed ~ N(0.53, 0.33²) s/step, clipped) or overtake diagonally if allowed, and (3) boards the head of the entry queue when a place on the first step is free. Groups and their formations, baggage, boarding hesitation, group breakup, the age mix and up/down direction follow Xie et al. (2022) and the thesis extensions E1–E7.

This page runs a JavaScript port of model.py, arrivals.py, runner.py and plos.py in your browser. The rules are the same; the random-number generator is different, so a given seed does not reproduce the Python run exactly.

Results

  • Throughput: exits per hour.
  • Queueing delay: joining the queue to stepping on; people still waiting at the end count with their wait so far.
  • Peak and mean queue: people waiting at the entry, sampled each second.
  • Saturated capacity (stand-only check): exits per hour when demand is unlimited, the practical capacity.
  • Clear difference: |B − A| larger than √(CIA² + CIB²), an approximate 5% test.

PLOS criteria, Yadav & Rastogi (2025)

v / Cf

People boarding in each 24-s interval, converted to ped/m/min (1 m wide escalator), divided by the reference capacity Cf. Cf = 172 ped/m/min at 0.65 m/s (88.4% of the theoretical 195) and is scaled the same way for other belt speeds. The escalator is graded on its peak interval.

Approach density

People waiting at the entry divided by the approach area (9 m², the paper's 8.84–9.38 m² trapezoid), every second. Graded on the maximum. The CA queue has no spatial extent, so densities above about 4 ped/m² mean the queue would spill beyond that area.

Overall PLOS

The worse of the two grades. Approach speed, the paper's third criterion, is not available because the CA does not model walking towards the escalator.
